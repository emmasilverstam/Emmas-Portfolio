A shared system for circular home styling

Type: Individual work

When: 2025-2026

Duration: Ten weeks, full time

Project scope: Master thesis in collaboartion with Invencon and Indie Studios

This master thesis explores how interaction design can support collaboration and reuse in a circular home styling workflow. The project was carried out together with the consultancy firm Invencon and their client Indie Studios, a home styling company that stages homes for real estate photography and viewings. Furniture is reused across many projects, which creates a strong need for coordination and overview. To support this circular way of working, Indie Studios has established Indie Home, their own store and warehouse responsible for storage, logistics and resale of styling furniture, while transport partners handle delivery and returns between projects. Today, the workflow involves several roles and relies heavily on manual coordination and separate tools. This makes it difficult to keep track of projects and products, and to support circular decisions in everyday work. The outcome of the project is a role-based mobile interface that supports coordination, product tracking and collaboration across roles.

The Problem

For the circular model to function in practice and make the workflow more efficient, the involved actors are highly dependent on a shared digital system. The intended workflow relies on close coordination between stylists, warehouse staff and transport, as well as continuous access to accurate information about projects and furniture across time.

From an interaction design perspective, this means supporting coordination of information, tasks and responsibilities across multiple roles. A digital system needs to enable collaboration, provide a shared project overview and support furniture traceability throughout the workflow. The diagram illustrates the intended workflow when supported by a shared digital interface.

While the circular workflow is well defined in theory, it is less clear how a digital system should be designed to support this collaboration in practice. This master’s thesis therefore addresses this challenge by exploring how a digital system can support coordination and collaboration in a circular home styling workflow.

Research questions

The following research questions were formulated to guide the project and frame the design of a digital system for a circular home styling workflow.

Q1

What are the main challenges and user requirements in circular home styling workflows involving multiple actors?

Q2

How can a mobile digital interface be designed to enhance coordination, communication and usability across the different roles in this workflow?

Design process

To investigate these questions and develop a design grounded in user needs and real work practices, the project followed an iterative, user-centred design approach based on the Design Thinking model. The process covered the phases Empathise, Define, Ideate, Prototype and Test, with continuous feedback from Indie Studios and their partners.

Old systems

During the Empathise phase, two previously developed digital systems for Indie Studios were reviewed. Both aimed to support parts of the circular workflow, but neither is used today. The system shown to the right, built in Power Apps, was used in practice and appreciated for its clear product overview. However, it lacked system integration, which led to manual work, and it was later discontinued when a collaborating furniture retailer went bankrupt. The system shown to the left aimed to support the full workflow and circular tracking, but was mainly backend-oriented and lacked sufficient UX/UI design. It was never fully implemented. Together, these systems highlighted key limitations in earlier solutions and informed important requirements for the new design.

Requirements list

Interviews were conducted across all roles to understand workflows, coordination and everyday challenges. These insights were synthesised into user journeys and translated into a prioritised requirements list using the MoSCoW method.

The Must-have requirements define the core of the system, focusing on shared project overviews, role-based access and furniture traceability. These include project management, shared checklists for packing, transport and returns, QR-based tracking, and support for reuse and resale. The Should- and Could-have requirements include additional planning and coordination features, such as calendars, notes, extended damage reporting and checklist printing, and were prioritised lower as they were not essential to the core workflow.

Design iterations

The design evolved through iterative ideation, sketching and prototyping. Early ideas were explored through hand-drawn sketches and low-fidelity wireframes to define structure, flows and role-based views. These were then developed into mid- and high-fidelity prototypes in Figma as the concept matured.

Two rounds of usability testing were conducted with participants from Indie Studios, Indie Home and transport roles. The first test focused on validating structure, terminology and workflow logic using a mid-fidelity prototype. The final test was conducted with an interactive high-fidelity prototype with interactive flows, enabling realistic testing of role-specific tasks such as project creation, packing, QR scanning, damage reporting and circular decisions. Insights from both tests informed subsequent design iterations and refinements.

The result

The project resulted in Indie Flow, a role-based mobile system designed to support collaboration in a circular home styling workflow. The system brings together project coordination, product tracking and circular handling in one shared interface used by stylists, warehouse staff and transport roles. By providing a shared overview of projects, clear role-specific tasks and traceability of furniture over time, the design supports smoother coordination across the workflow. Circular decisions such as reuse, resale and damage handling are integrated directly into everyday work, rather than handled as separate processes. The final interactive prototype demonstrates how a shared digital system can make a complex, multi-actor circular workflow more transparent, coordinated and manageable in practice.

Creating a new project

A project starts when a stylist at Indie Studios creates a new project in Indie Flow. The stylist enters basic project details, sets dates, delivery and return times, assigns responsibility and adds client information. Rooms are then created, and furniture is selected from the product list using search and filters. Products are assigned to rooms and reviewed in the packing list. When the project is sent, it becomes visible to Indie Home and the next phase of the workflow begins.

Packing and preparation

When a new project is created, Indie Home is notified and the project appears in their project overview, organised by status. Staff open projects marked as Ready to pack to access the packing list and full project details, including address, timings and notes. As items are collected in the warehouse, products are checked off manually or via QR scanning, with options for search, printed lists and bulk selection. Once all items are packed, the project is marked as ready to send and moves to the next phase for delivery.

Delivery and transport

When a project is ready to be delivered, it appears in the Indie Move interface, where transports are organised by upcoming deliveries and returns. On the delivery day, transport staff start the delivery by marking it as in progress. Once the transport begins, the stylist at Indie Studios is automatically notified that the delivery has left the warehouse and is on its way to the client.

Styling period

Once the furniture is delivered, the delivery is marked as completed and the stylist can begin the styling work. The items then remain in the residence for the duration of the styling period until it is time for return.

Return and inspection

When the return date arrives, Indie Move collects the furniture from the residence. Transport staff open the project under Upcoming returns and use the packing list to check off items as they are collected, either manually or via QR scanning with instant visual feedback. Any damages or deviations can be reported directly in the system and linked to a specific product using notes and photos. Once all items are checked off, the return is confirmed and the project becomes ready to be unpacked by Indie Home.

Unpacking and circular handling

Once the furniture is returned, the project is unpacked at Indie Home and items are placed back in the warehouse. From the product management view, staff can add or edit products, manage QR codes and move items to the circular store when needed. A dedicated circular section gathers items flagged due to usage or reported damages, supporting decisions on whether products remain in circulation or are moved to the circular store for resale.
